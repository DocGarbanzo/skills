How it works

This is a cross-coupled astable multivibrator. When Q1 saturates, its collector falls by about 5 V. C2 transfers that step to Q2's base and drives it to about −4.2 V, which cuts Q2 off. Q2's base then recovers through R4 toward +5 V and switches Q2 back on when it crosses VBE ≈ 0.65 V. The same process then runs on the other side. Each half period is t = R·C·ln((2VCC−VBE)/(VCC−VBE)) ≈ R·C·ln 2, so T ≈ 1.386 · 47 kΩ · 22 µF = 1.43 s. The simulation gives 1.36 s. To change the speed, change R3/R4 or C1/C2: doubling C halves the blink rate.

Before you power up

Transistor pinout. The layouts assume E-B-C pin order, read left to right with the flat face toward you, as on the 2N2222A, 2N3904, S8050 and S9013. The build order always lists a transistor's pins as E, B, C, so compare their column numbers. If E is in the lower-numbered column (E left of C), the flat face points toward the bottom edge of the board, toward you. If C is left of E, the flat face points toward the top edge. If your part is a BC547 or BC548 (C-B-E order), turn every transistor 180° from what the drawing shows. Check the part number printed on the flat face.
Electrolytic polarity. The + lead is the longer one; the stripe on the can marks −. In the astable, the + side of each timing capacitor goes to a collector. The base it drives swings to −4.2 V, which is within the transistor's 6 V reverse base limit.
If it doesn't work. If the LED stays dark, check the LED orientation, then the supply rails. If the LED stays lit but never blinks, the usual cause is a transistor in backwards or a capacitor in the wrong strip. Compare each lead against the hole list.
Layouts are hand-placed on the bblayout 0.0.1 engine and checked with its verifier for shorts, open nets and hole conflicts. Second simulations of the as-placed boards, including breadboard parasitics, matched the design simulations within 3 %.
